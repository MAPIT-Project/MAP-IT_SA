📝 QUESTION: What types of actions are available (RIA, IA, CSA)?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 1
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
The three main types of actions available in Horizon Europe are Research and Innovation Actions (RIA), Innovation Actions (IA), and Coordination and Support Actions (CSA). They differ primarily based on the Technology Readiness Level (TRL) of the work and the associated funding rates.

### Horizon Europe Action Types Summary

| Type | Full Name | TRL Range | Funding Rate | Primary Focus |
|------|-----------|--------------|--------------|---------------|
| **RIA** | Research and Innovation Action | TRL 2-5 | 100% | Focuses on advancing knowledge, developing new technology, or fundamental research. |
| **IA** | Innovation Action | TRL 5-8 | 70%/100% | Focuses on demonstration, piloting, testing, and scaling up solutions close to market deployment. (Note: Non-profit entities receive 100% funding; for-profit entities usually receive 70%). |
| **CSA** | Coordination and Support Action | N/A | 100% | Covers networking, standardisation, dissemination, communication, studies, and policy support. Does not fund research or demonstration activities directly. |

### Key Differentiators

*   **Risk vs. Market Proximity:** RIAs focus on higher-risk, fundamental steps (lower TRL), while IAs focus on lowering the final hurdles before commercialisation (higher TRL).
*   **Funding:** RIAs and CSAs always offer 100% reimbursement of eligible costs. IAs offer 70% for standard for-profit partners but 100% for non-profit entities (like universities or NGOs) and for SMEs if specified in the call.

📝 QUESTION: Are there specific co-funding or in-kind contribution requirements for Horizon Europe?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 2
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
Horizon Europe projects are primarily defined by the EU contribution rate applied to the eligible costs, and generally, there are **no specific mandatory co-funding requirements** imposed on top of this structure. However, there are clear rules governing how **in-kind contributions** are managed. 

### 1. Co-Funding (Financial Contribution)

In Horizon Europe, the concept is that the EU reimburses a percentage of the total eligible costs, rather than demanding a separate, mandatory financial co-funding target. 

| Action Type | EU Contribution Rate | Beneficiary Share (Co-funding) |
|-------------|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| **RIA/CSA (All Entities)** | 100% | 0% (of eligible costs)|
| **IA (Non-profit/Public)** | 100% | 0% (of eligible costs)|
| **IA (Profit-making)** | 70% | 30% (of eligible costs)|

*   **No Mandatory Co-financing:** For most research actions (RIA) and all non-profit entities, the EU covers 100% of the eligible costs, meaning there is no mandatory co-funding required.
*   **In-built Co-funding for IA:** For profit-making entities (e.g., large companies, or SMEs participating in Innovation Actions), the 70% rate automatically means the beneficiary must cover the remaining 30% of eligible costs. This 30% is their 'co-funding' contribution.

### 2. In-Kind Contributions

In-kind contributions refer to non-financial resources provided to the action, typically by third parties (who are not beneficiaries). These are generally **allowed** and can, in certain circumstances, be declared as **eligible costs**.

#### A. Eligible In-Kind Contributions

If a third party gives an in-kind contribution (such as personnel time, equipment, or research infrastructure access), the costs associated with that contribution can be considered eligible costs for the beneficiary, provided they meet strict conditions outlined in the Annotated Grant Agreement (AGA), including:

1.  They must be explicitly foreseen in Annex 1 (Description of the Action).
2.  They must fulfill all standard eligibility conditions (e.g., exclude ineligible costs, be necessary for the project).
3.  They must be properly documented and verifiable.

#### B. Non-Eligible In-Kind Contributions

Third parties are also permitted to provide voluntary in-kind contributions that are **not** declared as eligible costs by any beneficiary. While these resources are crucial for the project's success, they do not enter into the calculation of the final EU grant amount.

*Source: Horizon Europe Regulation, Annotated Grant Agreement (Article 6.2 and 9.2)*

📝 QUESTION: Can third-country participants join Horizon Europe projects?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 2
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
Yes, legal entities from third countries can participate in Horizon Europe projects. However, their participation conditions and eligibility for EU funding depend heavily on their country's status relative to the EU.

There are three main categories of participants:

### 1. EU Member States (MS)

*   **Eligibility:** Fully eligible and always fundable.

### 2. Associated Countries (AC)

Associated Countries (ACs) have finalized an agreement with the EU to contribute financially to Horizon Europe.

*   **Eligibility & Funding:** Legal entities from ACs participate under **equivalent conditions** as entities from EU Member States. They are fully eligible to coordinate and receive funding, unless specific limitations are set out in the Work Programme.

### 3. Other Third Countries (TCC) – Neither MS nor AC

Entities from countries that are neither EU MS nor ACs can still join consortia, but their funding eligibility follows specific rules:

#### A. Automatically Eligible for Funding

Entities established in **Low- and Middle-Income Countries (LMIC)**, as defined by the European Commission, are typically **automatically eligible for funding** from the EU budget when they participate in Horizon Europe projects.

#### B. Generally Not Eligible for Automatic Funding

Entities established in **High-Income Third Countries (HIC)** are generally **not automatically eligible for funding**. They can participate, but must typically cover their own costs. This includes countries like the USA, Canada, Japan, Australia, etc.

**Exceptions to the HIC Funding Rule:**

High-Income Third Country participants *may* receive EU funding in specific, limited circumstances, such as:

1.  When funding is **specifically provided for** in the relevant Work Programme or Call Topic.
2.  When their participation is **deemed essential** for carrying out the action (e.g., possessing unique expertise, infrastructure, or access to data).
3.  For certain specific actions, such as ERC grants or MSCA, where specific rules apply.

---

### Summary of Funding Rules

| Country Type | Participation Allowed? | Automatic EU Funding? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EU Member State (MS)** | Yes | Yes (100% or 70%) |
| **Associated Country (AC)** | Yes | Yes (Equivalent to MS) |
| **Low/Middle Income TCC** | Yes | Yes |
| **High Income TCC** | Yes | No (Unless specifically requested by the EC/topic) |

*Source: Horizon Europe Programme Guide, General Annexes*

📝 QUESTION: How does Horizon Europe align with the Green Deal?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 2
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
Horizon Europe (HE) is the European Union’s main research and innovation instrument for implementing the ambitious goals of the **European Green Deal (EGD)**, which aims to make Europe the first climate-neutral continent by 2050. The entire framework is structured to ensure that research priorities directly translate into Green Deal objectives. 

### Key Mechanisms for Alignment

The alignment is enforced through strategic planning, dedicated clusters, and novel instruments like the EU Missions, backed by specific budgetary targets.

#### 1. Financial Commitment (The 35% Target)

Horizon Europe has a statutory objective to dedicate **at least 35% of its total budget** to climate-related objectives. This ensures that climate action is mainstreamed across all relevant parts of the programme, particularly Pillar II (Global Challenges and European Industrial Competitiveness).

#### 2. Pillar II Clusters

The six clusters under Pillar II are primarily designed to address the socio-economic and environmental challenges identified by the EGD. The most directly aligned clusters are:

*   **Cluster 5: Climate, Energy and Transport:** This cluster is the core instrument for developing innovative solutions to decarbonize the energy and transport sectors, focusing on renewable energies, sustainable mobility, climate science, and circular industries.
*   **Cluster 6: Food, Bioeconomy, Natural Resources, Agriculture and Environment:** Focuses on implementing the 'Farm to Fork' strategy, protecting biodiversity, reducing pollution (zero-pollution ambition), and managing natural resources sustainably.

#### 3. EU Missions

The five EU Missions are novel portfolio instruments within Horizon Europe designed to deliver bold, inspirational, and measurable societal impact within a set timeframe. Three of the five Missions are directly linked to the Green Deal:

| Mission Title | Green Deal Priority | Focus Area |
|--------------------------------------|----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **100 Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities** | Climate Neutrality | Supports cities in becoming climate-neutral by 2030, acting as innovation hubs. |
| **Restore our Ocean and Waters** | Zero Pollution, Biodiversity | Restoring aquatic ecosystems, reducing plastic pollution, and making the blue economy sustainable. |
| **A Soil Deal for Europe** | Farm to Fork, Biodiversity | Ensures 75% of European soils are healthy by 2030 for food, people, nature, and climate.

In essence, Horizon Europe provides the necessary funding and framework to develop the technologies, social innovations, and regulatory support required to achieve the European Green Deal's environmental and climate targets.

💬 RESPONSE:
Horizon Europe incorporates several cross-cutting priorities that applicants must address in their proposals and implementation, reflecting the EU's commitment to research excellence, societal values, and policy goals. The two most prominent horizontal priorities are **Open Science** and **Gender Equality**.

***

## 1. Open Science

Open Science is mandated by default in Horizon Europe and moves beyond just Open Access publishing. It aims to ensure that research outcomes (publications, data, methods) are openly available, accessible, and reusable.

### Key Pillars of Open Science Requirements:

| Requirement | Details |
|---|---|
| **Open Access to Publications** | Mandatory. Researchers must ensure that peer-reviewed scientific publications are immediately accessible upon publication (Zero embargo period) and provided under a Creative Commons Attribution Licence (CC BY) or equivalent. |
| **Research Data Management** | Beneficiaries must manage the research data generated according to the **FAIR principles** (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable). This includes preparing and implementing a Data Management Plan (DMP). Data needs to be open by default, though exceptions (for IPR, privacy, or security reasons) can be justified. |
| **Responsible Research & Innovation (RRI)** | While not a strict funding requirement for all actions, RRI principles—including public engagement, ethics, and research integrity—are strongly encouraged and often assessed as part of the 'Impact' criteria. |

***

## 2. Gender Equality

Gender equality is addressed through two compulsory requirements: ensuring gender balance within the consortium team and addressing the gender dimension in the research content itself, alongside a specific administrative eligibility criterion.

### Mandatory Eligibility Requirement: Gender Equality Plan (GEP)

For calls closing in 2022 onwards, organizations established in EU Member States and Associated Countries that are **public bodies, research organizations, or higher education institutions (HEIs)** must have a dedicated, publicly available **Gender Equality Plan (GEP)** in place to be eligible for funding. This is a mandatory administrative requirement.

### Requirements in Proposal Evaluation:

| Area | Description |
|---|---|
| **Gender Balance in the Consortium** | Applicants must aim for gender balance at all levels of personnel assigned to the action, including supervisory and managerial roles. This is assessed under the **Implementation** criterion. |
| **Integrating the Gender Dimension in R&I Content** | Proposals must analyze whether and how sex and/or gender are relevant variables in the research or innovation content. Failing to adequately address the gender dimension when it is relevant can significantly lower the score under the **Excellence** criterion. This aims to eliminate bias and ensure research outcomes benefit all citizens. |

*Sources: Horizon Europe Programme Guide, EU Grants Annotated Grant Agreement (AGA), Built-in Knowledge Base*

📝 QUESTION: What are the reporting requirements for Horizon Europe projects?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 2
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
Horizon Europe projects require two main streams of reporting: **Continuous Reporting** throughout the project lifecycle, and **Periodic Reporting** at predefined intervals to trigger interim and final payments.

### 1. Continuous Reporting

Continuous reporting is conducted throughout the project duration via the EU Funding & Tenders Portal (Continuous Reporting Module). It keeps the granting authority updated on operational progress and administrative changes.

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| **Milestones & Deliverables** | Ongoing submission of completed milestones and deliverables according to the schedule set out in Annex 1. |
| **Risks** | Updates on critical risks encountered and the mitigation strategies implemented. |
| **Publications & IPR** | Reporting on publications, communication activities, intellectual property rights (IPR), and dissemination efforts. |
| **Summary** | Regular updates to the publishable summary. |
| **Programme-Specific Info** | Reporting on indicators related to cross-cutting issues like Gender, Open Data, Training, etc., as required by the specific action type. |

### 2. Periodic Reporting (Technical and Financial)

Periodic Reports are mandatory submissions required by the coordinator at the end of each reporting period defined in the Grant Agreement. They serve as the prerequisite for receiving interim payments.

#### A. Technical Report

This part focuses on the achievements of the project during the reporting period:

*   **Progress Summary:** Detailed narrative of the work carried out, objectives achieved, and deviations from the original plan (if any).
*   **Impact:** Updates on the project's contribution towards the expected impacts, including relevant KPIs and programme-specific indicators.
*   **Use of Resources:** Explanation of how the project utilized resources.

#### B. Financial Report (Form C)

This part summarizes the eligible costs incurred by *all* partners during the reporting period.

*   **Cost Declaration:** Each beneficiary submits its own financial statement (Form C) detailing personnel costs, travel costs, equipment, subcontracting, and other direct and indirect costs.
*   **Payment Request:** The financial report determines the amount of the interim payment requested.

### Reporting Deadlines

The coordinator must submit the Periodic Report to the European Commission/Funding Agency **within 60 days** after the end of the relevant reporting period (often 18 months or 12 months, as specified in the Grant Agreement).

### Final Report

The final reporting period typically includes both the Technical and Financial Reports submitted together within 60 days of the project end date, followed by a final review and payment.

***Source: EU Funding & Tenders Online Manual, Annotated Grant Agreement***
