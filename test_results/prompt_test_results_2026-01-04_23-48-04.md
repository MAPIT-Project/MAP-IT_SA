## General Introduction

### Question 1

**Question:**

What is the structure of the Horizon Europe programme?

**Response:**

Horizon Europe is the EU's main funding programme for research and innovation (2021-2027), structured around three core pillars designed to address global challenges and boost European competitiveness. The programme has an overall budget of approximately **€95.5 billion**.

Here is the detailed structure of the Horizon Europe programme:

---

## 🏛️ Pillar I: Excellent Science (€25.8 Billion)

**Focus:** Strengthening the EU's scientific base and securing world-class research talent.

| Component | Focus/Goal |
|---|---|
| **European Research Council (ERC)** | Funds frontier, high-risk/high-gain research defined by the Principal Investigator (PI), across all fields. Actions: Starting, Consolidator, Advanced, and Synergy Grants. |
| **Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA)** | Funds training, mobility, and career development for researchers at all stages (pre-doctoral to established). Actions: Postdoctoral Fellowships (PF), Doctoral Networks (DN). |
| **Research Infrastructures** | Developing, consolidating, and operating world-class research infrastructures, including digital services. |

## 🌍 Pillar II: Global Challenges and European Industrial Competitiveness (€53.5 Billion)

**Focus:** Supporting research focusing on societal challenges and industrial technologies, defined through cooperation across six thematic clusters and five specific EU Missions.

### Thematic Clusters:

1. **Health (CL1):** Health systems, non-communicable and rare diseases, environmental determinants of health.
2. **Culture, Creativity and Inclusive Society (CL2):** Democracy, cultural heritage, and social transformations.
3. **Civil Security for Society (CL3):** Disaster resilience, border management, and cybersecurity.
4. **Digital, Industry and Space (CL4):** Manufacturing, digital technologies (AI, data), and key enabling technologies.
5. **Climate, Energy and Mobility (CL5):** Climate science, renewable energy, energy systems, and sustainable transport.
6. **Food, Bioeconomy, Natural Resources, and Agriculture & Environment (CL6):** Biodiversity, sustainable food systems, and circular economy.

### The EU Missions
These are bold, inspiring goals aiming to deliver concrete results by 2030, covering five areas:

*   Cancer
*   Adaptation to Climate Change
*   Healthy Oceans, Seas, Coastal and Inland Waters
*   Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities
*   Soil Health and Food

## 🚀 Pillar III: Innovative Europe (€13.6 Billion)

**Focus:** Fostering innovation, market deployment, and scaling up high-potential technologies and SMEs.

| Component | Focus/Goal |
|---|---|
| **European Innovation Council (EIC)** | Supports breakthrough and disruptive innovation, focusing on deep tech. Provides funding through grants (e.g., EIC Pathfinder for early TRL) and blended finance (EIC Accelerator for market-ready innovations). |
| **European Innovation Ecosystems (EIE)** | Connecting regional and national innovation actors to strengthen ecosystems across the EU. |
| **European Institute of Innovation & Technology (EIT)** | Integrating research, education, and innovation through Knowledge and Innovation Communities (KICs). |

Horizon Europe also includes a dedicated component addressing structural weaknesses in the EU research landscape, including support for Member States lagging in R&I performance (Widening Participation) and initiatives to deepen the European Research Area (ERA).

### Question 4

**Question:**

What are instruments (RIA, CSA, IA etc)?

**Response:**

The instruments you listed (RIA, IA, CSA) are the core 'Action Types' used within Horizon Europe. They define the nature of the project, the expected Technology Readiness Level (TRL) it should address, and the applicable funding rate.

Here is a summary of the main instruments:

### Horizon Europe Action Types

| Type | Full Name | TRL Range | Funding Rate | Focus & Objective |
|------|-----------|--------------|--------------|----------------------|
| **RIA** | Research and Innovation Action | TRL 2-5 | 100% | **Focus:** Research. Activities aimed at establishing new knowledge, exploring new technologies, or improving existing ones. The goal is to advance fundamental scientific understanding. |
| **IA** | Innovation Action | TRL 5-8 | 70%/100% | **Focus:** Demonstration and Piloting. Activities primarily focused on demonstrating the viability of a new solution or technology close to market. Funding is generally **70%** for for-profit entities (SMEs, Large Companies) and **100%** for non-profit entities (Universities, Research Institutes, Public Bodies). |
| **CSA** | Coordination and Support Action | N/A | 100% | **Focus:** Networking, Communication, and Coordination. Activities that do not involve significant research or innovation but instead support existing or future research activities, such as setting up networks, standardisation, studies, or awareness-raising. |

### Key Differences

1.  **Technology Readiness Level (TRL):** RIA focuses on the early stages of research (low TRL), while IA focuses on bringing proven concepts closer to the market (high TRL).
2.  **Funding Rate:** RIA and CSA almost always receive 100% funding of eligible costs. IA has a split rate, penalizing commercial partners (SMEs/Large Companies) to encourage market readiness and co-investment.
3.  **Page Limits:** Proposals for RIA/IA are significantly longer (45 pages) than those for CSA (30 pages), reflecting the deeper technical detail required for R&D and demonstration projects.

### Question 5

**Question:**

What will come after Horizon Europe?

**Response:**

The successor to the current Horizon Europe programme (which runs until the end of 2027) is generally referred to as the **10th Framework Programme (FP10)**.

This programme will cover the period of the next Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF), running from **2028 to 2034**.

While the detailed legal proposals and final budget are still under development and require approval by the European Parliament and Council, the preparation phase (often termed the 'Strategic Debate on the Future of EU R&I') is already well underway.

### Key Principles and Anticipated Focus Areas for FP10

The preparation for FP10 is heavily focused on learning lessons from Horizon 2020 and Horizon Europe, particularly regarding administrative burden, impact realization, and global competitiveness. Key themes expected to shape FP10 include:

1.  **Simplification and Speed:** A primary goal is to drastically simplify the application process, reporting requirements, and grant management, aiming for faster time-to-grant and better usability for applicants.
2.  **Geopolitical Focus:** The programme is expected to strengthen Europe's technological sovereignty and economic security, potentially incorporating specific measures for critical technologies (e.g., microelectronics, AI, quantum) and reducing strategic dependencies.
3.  **Digital and Green Transitions:** These twin transitions (Green Deal and Digital Decade) will remain central organizing principles, likely continuing the clustering approach from Horizon Europe.
4.  **Strengthening ERA and Global Role:** Continuing the work on the European Research Area (ERA) agenda, promoting deeper institutional reforms, and enhancing the EU's role in global R&I cooperation (while balancing strategic autonomy).
5.  **Evolution of Core Instruments:**
    *   **EIC Accelerator:** Expected to continue and potentially be reinforced, focusing on highly impactful, market-creating innovation and blended finance.
    *   **ERC:** The European Research Council's excellence-based frontier research model is considered highly successful and is expected to remain largely unchanged, possibly with increased budget allocation.
    *   **Missions and Partnerships:** Both the EU Missions (focused on concrete, measurable goals like climate neutrality or cancer) and the European Partnerships are anticipated to evolve, potentially with more stringent governance and closer alignment to political priorities.

### Timeline for FP10 Preparation

*   **2023 – 2025:** High-level strategic consultations, reports (e.g., from dedicated expert groups), and public feedback campaigns take place.
*   **Late 2025 – Early 2026:** The European Commission is expected to publish its formal legislative proposal for FP10 and the next MFF, launching the legislative negotiation phase.
*   **2026 – 2027:** Negotiations occur between the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union.
*   **2028:** FP10 is scheduled to officially start (assuming legislative approval is finalized).

### Question 12

**Question:**

Has there ever been a project dealing with the topic of circular economy? Please present those projects in 2-2 paragraphs.

**Response:**

Yes, the Circular Economy (CE) is a major cross-cutting priority within Horizon Europe, leading to the funding of numerous large-scale research and innovation actions (RIA/IA) as well as strategic individual grants (MSCA, ERC).

Here are examples of funded projects, grouped by their specific focus area:

### 1. Circularity in Critical Raw Materials and Infrastructure

A significant portion of CE funding targets the technical challenges of resource efficiency, particularly for high-value or difficult-to-recycle materials. A key project in this area is **EFESTO4EU** (*Effective recycling of Rare Earth Elements from End-of-Life products for European Circular Economy*). This project addresses the EU's dependence on imported Critical Raw Materials (CRMs) by developing non-conventional, cost-effective recycling methods for Rare Earth Elements (REEs) found in end-of-life products (like electronics). The goal is to close the material loop for these strategic components, which are vital for clean technologies and the green transition. Similarly, the project **GOLFatDue** (*Advancing Green Long-life Roadways*) focuses on making infrastructure circular by enhancing the durability and service life of sustainable fiber-reinforced recycled asphalt. By focusing on long-life road materials, this research promotes a circular economy in the construction sector, reducing both raw material extraction and associated CO2 emissions.

### 2. Systemic Modelling and Climate Mitigation Integration

Beyond specific material flows, other projects focus on developing the necessary economic and policy tools to understand and implement circularity at a systemic level.
