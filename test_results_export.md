📝 QUESTION: What types of actions are available (RIA, IA, CSA)?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 4
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
The three most common types of funding actions available in Horizon Europe (and other EU Framework Programmes) are Research and Innovation Actions (RIA), Innovation Actions (IA), and Coordination and Support Actions (CSA).

Here is a breakdown of what each action type entails:

### 1. Research and Innovation Actions (RIA)

*   **Focus:** Establishing new knowledge and exploring the feasibility of a new or improved technology, product, process, service, or solution.
*   **Activities:** Basic and applied research, technology development and integration, testing and validation on a small-scale prototype in a laboratory or simulated environment.
*   **Technology Readiness Level (TRL):** Typically targets TRL 1 to TRL 5/6.
*   **Funding Rate:** Generally **100%** of eligible costs.

### 2. Innovation Actions (IA)

*   **Focus:** Activities that are closer to the market, involving large-scale demonstration, prototyping, testing, and validation of new or improved solutions under real-world operating conditions.
*   **Activities:** Piloting, demonstrating, testing, and scaling up.
*   **Technology Readiness Level (TRL):** Typically targets TRL 6 to TRL 8.
*   **Funding Rate:** Generally **70%** of eligible costs (except for non-profit legal entities, where the rate is 100%).

### 3. Coordination and Support Actions (CSA)

*   **Focus:** Activities aimed at coordinating or supporting research and innovation activities (e.g., networking, knowledge dissemination, awareness-raising, standardisation, or policy dialogue).
*   **Activities:** Communication, conferences, workshops, studies, road-mapping, data collection, and access to research infrastructures.
*   **TRL:** CSAs do **not** directly fund research or innovation activities.
*   **Funding Rate:** Generally **100%** of eligible costs.

📝 QUESTION: Can third-country participants join Horizon Europe projects?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 3
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
Yes, third-country participants can join Horizon Europe projects, but their eligibility for EU funding depends significantly on their country's status. Participation rules are generally divided into three categories:

### 1. Associated Countries (AC)

Entities legally established in a country 'associated' with Horizon Europe are treated almost identically to those in EU Member States. 

*   **Participation:** Fully eligible to join consortia.
*   **Funding:** **Automatically eligible for EU funding** under the same conditions as Member State entities (e.g., 100% funding for RIA, 70% for IA/100% for non-profit).

### 2. Low-to-Middle Income Countries (LMICs)

Legal entities established in specific third countries classified as Low- or Middle-Income Countries (as defined by the OECD) are generally automatically eligible for EU funding.

*   **Participation:** Fully eligible to join consortia.
*   **Funding:** **Automatically eligible for EU funding**.

### 3. Non-Associated, High-Income Countries

Entities from other third countries (i.e., countries that are neither EU Member States, nor associated, nor LMICs, such as the US, Canada, Japan, etc.) are subject to stricter rules:

*   **Participation:** They are generally allowed to participate in projects (provided the minimum consortium requirements are met by EU Member States/Associated Countries).
*   **Funding:** **They are generally NOT eligible for EU funding** and must participate at their own cost (using their own funds or national funding schemes).

#### Exceptions to the Non-Funding Rule:

The EU may grant funding to entities from non-associated high-income countries in specific, exceptional cases, such as when:

1.  Their participation is **deemed essential** for carrying out the action (e.g., providing unique expertise, accessing specialized infrastructure).
2.  The specific **Work Programme topic explicitly allows** for their funding.

📝 QUESTION: What are the cross-cutting priorities like open science and gender in Horizon Europe?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 4
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
Horizon Europe is designed to mainstream ethical and societal priorities, known as **cross-cutting issues**, throughout all its funding activities. The two most prominent priorities are **Gender Equality** and **Open Science**.

***

### 1. Gender Equality

Gender equality is a fundamental value of the EU and a cross-cutting priority integrated into all stages of the research cycle, from work programme design to project implementation.

#### Mandatory Gender Equality Plans (GEP)

The most significant new requirement in Horizon Europe is the mandatory **Gender Equality Plan (GEP)**. To be eligible for funding, certain types of organizations must have a GEP in place by the time of the call deadline (for calls with deadlines in 2022 and beyond).

**Who needs a GEP?**
*   **Public bodies** (including national, regional, and local government bodies)
*   **Research organizations**
*   **Higher education establishments**

This requirement applies to entities established in EU Member States and Associated Countries. The GEP must meet minimum process and substance requirements, including:
*   Publication on the organization's website.
*   Dedicated resources and expertise for implementation.
*   Data collection and monitoring based on gender disaggregated data.
*   Training and awareness-raising.

**Integration into Research**
Gender dimension must also be included in the content of the research and innovation proposals (Gender as a Research Content). This is a criterion evaluated during the proposal assessment process.

***

### 2. Open Science

Horizon Europe makes Open Science the *modus operandi* of the programme. Open Science practices are mandatory, aiming to ensure publicly funded research results are openly accessible, reusable, and reproducible.

#### Key Requirements

1.  **Open Access to Publications:** Beneficiaries must ensure immediate Open Access (OA) to all peer-reviewed scientific publications resulting from the project. There is no longer an embargo period.

2.  **Research Data Management (FAIR Data):** Projects must adhere to the principles of **FAIR** (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) data management. This includes:
    *   Submitting a **Data Management Plan (DMP)** within the first six months of the project.
    *   Depositing research data in a trustworthy repository and ensuring it is openly accessible (as open as possible, as closed as necessary).

3.  **Inclusion in Proposals:** Proposers must detail their planned Open Science practices (including data management and data sharing) within the proposal. If **Open Science practices are not applicable** to the proposal (e.g., due to the nature of the research), **justification must be provided** for evaluation.

💬 RESPONSE:
Innovation Actions (IA) in Horizon Europe typically require a **high Technology Readiness Level (TRL)**, generally starting around **TRL 6** and aiming to reach **TRL 8** by the end of the project. 

Innovation Actions focus on activities close to market uptake, demonstrating and piloting new solutions in operational environments, which distinguishes them from actions focused purely on research.

***

### TRL Requirements by Action Type

To provide context, the different types of Horizon Europe actions are defined by the TRL range they cover:

1.  **Research and Innovation Actions (RIA):** These actions cover activities from basic research up to demonstrating a prototype system in a relevant environment. Their TRL range typically starts low (around TRL 2-3) and goes up to **TRL 5**.

2.  **Innovation Actions (IA):** These actions pick up where RIAs leave off. They focus on demonstration, piloting, testing, and validating solutions at a large scale. The starting point is usually a validated technology, covering **TRL 6 (technology demonstrated in relevant environment) up to TRL 8 (system completed and qualified)**.

3.  **Coordination and Support Actions (CSA):** These actions are TRL-agnostic, as they focus on networking, standardisation, dissemination, and policy support, not direct R&D or innovation deployment.

📝 QUESTION: What are the reporting requirements for Horizon Europe projects?
------------------------------------------------------------
✅ STATUS: Success
📊 Confidence: 0.8, Iterations: 2
------------------------------------------------------------
💬 RESPONSE:
Horizon Europe projects require a multi-faceted approach to reporting, divided into three main categories: Continuous Reporting, Periodic Reporting (Financial), and Scientific Reporting (Progress Reports).

### 1. Continuous Reporting
This type of reporting provides regular updates on the project's operational status and is done throughout the project lifecycle.

*   **Scope:** Continuous reporting focuses on the status of milestones and deliverables.
*   **Frequency:** Updates are expected regularly, though specific intervals depend on the project's operational schedule.
*   **Submission:** Submitted through the Funding & Tenders Portal.

### 2. Periodic Financial Reporting
This corresponds to the financial reporting requirements found in standard EU grants and is used to account for expenditure and request interim payments.

*   **Scope:** Detailed financial statements of costs incurred by all beneficiaries during the reporting period.
*   **Frequency:** Typically occurs at set intervals, such as every 18 months (e.g., Month 18, 36, 54, and 72 for longer projects).
*   **Content:** Financial statements, Certificates on Financial Statements (CFS) if thresholds are met, and a request for the interim payment.

### 3. Scientific Reporting (Progress Reports)
Scientific reporting is distinct from financial reporting and focuses specifically on the technical and scientific progress achieved during the reporting period.

*   **Scope:** Information about the scientific and technical work performed, results achieved, and fulfillment of objectives.
*   **Frequency:** Aligned with the periodic financial reporting deadlines.
*   **Submission:** Must be submitted through the Portal's Scientific Reporting tool.

All reports are submitted by the Coordinator on behalf of the consortium via the Funding & Tenders Portal.
